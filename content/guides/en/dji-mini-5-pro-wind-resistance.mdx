DJI's specs page lists the Mini 5 Pro, which weighs 249.9 g, with a maximum wind speed resistance of 12 m/s (43.2 km/h). That is the manufacturer's ceiling for a very small aircraft, not a promise that a windy day will be pleasant. Below: what DJI says about wind, how the Plus battery changes the other specs, and how FlyDroneMap grades gusts for this model.

## Key takeaways

- DJI's specs page lists a maximum wind speed resistance of 12 m/s (43.2 km/h) for the Mini 5 Pro.
- DJI's manual says not to fly in severe weather such as strong winds, snow, rain and fog, and not on rainy or windy days. In the parts we read it states no numeric wind limit for flight.
- For this model FlyDroneMap rates gusts under 21.6 km/h as good, 21.6 km/h and above as caution and 30.2 km/h and above as not recommended. Those lines belong to the site.
- Gusts and wind at flight height are the numbers to read.

## What the manual says about wind

Six points matter here.

- Do not fly in severe weather such as strong winds, snow, rain and fog, or on rainy or windy days.
- In strong wind the aircraft may be unable to return to the Home Point.
- During Return-to-Home it adjusts its flight speed on its own, according to wind speed, wind direction and obstacles.
- The one numeric wind figure we found is for Return-to-Home when satellite positioning was unavailable at takeoff: environmental wind speed should be below 3 m/s.
- In high wind, flight speed is limited further, and the gimbal may vibrate while recording in strong wind.
- Reduced battery capacity at low temperatures lowers the aircraft's wind speed resistance.

## Two batteries, two sets of figures

DJI's page gives different figures depending on the battery, and says the flight time numbers were measured without wind at sea level. Sport-mode speeds were measured in a wind tunnel and are reference values.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Max wind speed resistance | 12 m/s (43.2 km/h) |
| Takeoff weight | 249.9 g (the page notes an actual weight of about 249.9 g ± 4 g) |
| Max horizontal speed | 18 m/s in Sport mode with the standard battery (19 m/s with the Plus battery); 15 m/s in Tracking mode |
| Max flight time | 36 minutes with the Intelligent Flight Battery; 52 minutes with the Intelligent Flight Battery Plus, which DJI says is not sold in the EU. Measured with no wind at sea level, at a constant 21.6 km/h (25.2 km/h for the Plus battery) |
| Operational ceiling above sea level | 6,000 m with the standard battery; 4,500 m with the Plus battery |
| Operating temperature | -10 to 40 °C |
| Obstacle sensing | Omnidirectional dual-vision system, a forward LiDAR and a downward infrared sensor |
| Satellite positioning | GPS, Galileo and BeiDou |

Fitting the Plus battery changes several of these numbers. Rules that depend on weight differ by country and by your exact configuration, so check your setup against your national regulator.

## Reading a forecast against 43.2 km/h

FlyDroneMap compares the forecast gust with the official maximum: good below 50%, caution from 50%, not recommended from 70%.

| Verdict | Share of 43.2 km/h | Gust speed |
|---|---|---|
| Good | Under 50% | Under 21.6 km/h (6.0 m/s) |
| Caution | 50% to under 70% | 21.6 to under 30.2 km/h |
| Not recommended | 70% or more | 30.2 km/h and above |

The gust-risk scale on the dashboard is separate. It runs low under 20 km/h, moderate from 20 to under 35 km/h and high from 35 km/h, and it describes the air rather than the aircraft.

## Small aircraft, short margins

A light aircraft feels every gust as a push, and the official maximum says little about how that feels in the sticks. Forecast wind is usually given 10 m above ground, and at 80–120 m it may be much stronger; see [wind shear by altitude](/en/guides/wind-shear-by-altitude-and-drone-flight). Fighting a headwind costs battery, so a tailwind outbound leaves you with the hard part on the way home. Cold shrinks both the battery and the wind margin.

## Checklist before takeoff

1. Choose the Mini 5 Pro on the [FlyDroneMap dashboard](/en) and read the gust figure instead of the average.
2. Look at the wind at 80 and 120 m as well as the surface value.
3. Work through the [wind and gust checklist](/en/guides/wind-gust-checklist-before-drone-flight), including direction and nearby obstacles.
4. Plan to end the flight with the wind behind you, or with a generous battery reserve.
5. Compare other aircraft in [wind resistance by model](/en/guides/drone-wind-resistance-by-model).

## Pitfalls

- Reading 12 m/s as a comfortable cruising wind.
- Checking the average and skipping the gusts.
- Forgetting that the surface forecast does not describe the wind at flight height.
- Launching with a cold battery and expecting full wind performance.
- Drifting far downwind without working out the return.

## FAQ

### What is the wind limit of the DJI Mini 5 Pro?

DJI's specs page lists a maximum wind speed resistance of 12 m/s (43.2 km/h), and its manual advises against flying in severe weather such as strong winds.

### Can I fly the Mini 5 Pro in 25 km/h wind?

25 km/h is about 58% of 43.2 km/h, which FlyDroneMap rates as caution. Check whether the figure is a gust or an average and what the wind is at flight height.

### Does cold weather change the wind limit?

DJI's manual says reduced battery capacity in the cold lowers the aircraft's wind speed resistance but gives no number, so allow for a smaller margin.
