A drone can fly perfectly and still deliver unusable footage because of its gimbal. Most gimbal trouble starts with small handling mistakes between flights, not with a dramatic crash. This guide relies on DJI's published support pages and the DJI Mini 4 Pro manual as examples of what a manufacturer says. Other brands and models differ, so check your own manual. Where we give general camera-care advice that does not come from a drone manufacturer, we say so.

## Key takeaways

- The Mini 4 Pro manual tells you to remove the gimbal protector before powering on, because the self-diagnostic may otherwise be affected, and to fit the protector again when the aircraft is not in use.
- Its pre-flight checklist includes confirming that the gimbal and camera work normally and that the camera lenses and sensors are clean.
- If the gimbal looks skewed at power-on or the app shows "Gimbal Calibration Required", DJI's calibration guide points to the auto calibration in DJI Fly.
- DJI's page on shaking gimbals lists foreign objects, damaged rubber dampers, bent propellers, aggressive stick input in Sport mode, strong wind and calibration as things to check, and says to send the aircraft in after a fall or collision.
- Clean the lens with a blower, a soft brush and a microfiber cloth, in that order, and never with your breath or ordinary tissues.

## Check the gimbal before every flight

The manual of the DJI Mini 4 Pro includes the gimbal in its pre-flight checklist, asking you to confirm that the gimbal and camera work normally and that all lenses and sensors are clean. Make that a routine: power on, wait for the gimbal to level itself, then tilt the camera through its range in the app and watch for stalls, noise or a tilted horizon. A short check on the ground is cheaper than a ruined shot.

## Gimbal protector: off for takeoff, on for storage

The manual says to remove the gimbal protector before powering on the aircraft, because the self-diagnostic may be affected if it is still fitted, and it recommends fitting the gimbal protector and the propeller holder when the aircraft is not in use. DJI's page on shaking gimbals also lists the protector, the gimbal clamp and the vibration-absorbing foam pads as foreign objects to remove before powering on. Putting the protector on whenever the drone goes into a bag is a cheap habit, and it is the simplest way to keep sideways knocks away from the camera.

## A tilted horizon: calibrate before you worry

DJI's calibration guide says to calibrate when the gimbal looks skewed after power-on or when the app shows a "Gimbal Calibration Required" warning. In DJI Fly the steps are: power on the aircraft and remote controller, open the camera view, tap the three dots in the upper right corner to open System Settings, choose Control, then Gimbal Calibration, and select Auto. Menu names can change with app versions, so follow the prompts on your screen and keep the aircraft still while it runs. If the horizon stays skewed or the gimbal makes noise after calibration, treat it as a hardware problem and contact support.

## Shaky or erratic gimbal: a short checklist

DJI's support page on gimbal shake lists the following checks. Before powering on, remove anything that could block the gimbal, such as the protector or clamp. Inspect the rubber dampers; if any is broken, or if the aircraft has had a fall or collision, send it in for testing and repair. Replace any propeller that is bent or broken. During flight, strong wind can shake the aircraft and the image transmission, and Sport mode combined with full stick input can cause gimbal shake, so use gentler stick movements or switch to Normal or Cine mode. In settings, run the gimbal auto calibration. If none of this helps, submit a repair request.

## Dust, sand and moisture

The Mini 4 Pro manual tells you to fly in a dry environment and lists dust, storms, salt spray and fungus among the conditions to avoid, and it does not state a dust or water resistance rating for the aircraft in the pages we reviewed. We therefore keep the advice general: avoid taking off from sand or loose dust, because grit near moving parts is never helpful, and after a flight in such conditions check the gimbal for noise or roughness before the next flight. Take off from a landing pad or a clean surface when you can.

## Cleaning the lens

The manual asks you to confirm that the lenses are clean but the pages we reviewed do not describe a cleaning method, so check the maintenance section of your own manual. As general camera-care advice, StudioBinder recommends this order: a blower to remove loose dust, a soft brush for stubborn specks, and a microfiber cloth or lens tissue, with a drop of lens cleaner applied to the cloth rather than to the lens. It warns against blowing on the lens, since breath adds condensation, and against ordinary tissues, which can scratch, and it adds that cleaning too often can also harm the lens. Be extra gentle around the gimbal joints and do not press on the camera.

## Common mistake: assuming a bump did no harm

After a knock, a fall or a rough landing, do a gimbal check before the next flight, even if everything looks fine. Rubber dampers and thin camera cables are easy to overlook. If something sounds or feels wrong, the manufacturer's advice is to have the aircraft tested.

## Practical checklist

- Remove the gimbal protector before powering on, and fit it whenever the drone is stored or carried.
- Before flying, confirm that the gimbal moves freely, the horizon is level and the lens is clean.
- Run the auto calibration if the gimbal looks skewed or the app asks for it.
- If footage shakes, check for foreign objects, dampers and propellers, wind and Sport mode.
- Clean the lens with a blower, a brush and a microfiber cloth, never with breath or tissues.
- After a fall or collision, have the aircraft tested.

## FAQ

### Is a tilted horizon a sign of a broken gimbal?

Not necessarily. DJI says to calibrate when the gimbal looks skewed after power-on. If noise or a stuck gimbal remains, contact support.

### Do I need the gimbal protector for short trips?

The Mini 4 Pro manual recommends fitting it when the aircraft is not in use. It is a simple precaution for any transport.

### Why does my footage shake?

DJI lists foreign objects, damaged dampers, bent propellers, wind, Sport mode with strong stick input and calibration as things to check.

### How should I clean the lens?

Use a blower, a soft brush and a microfiber cloth, with a drop of cleaner on the cloth if needed, and avoid breath and ordinary tissues.
