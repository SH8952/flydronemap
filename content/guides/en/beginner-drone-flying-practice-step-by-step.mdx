Many early crashes come from skipping a step: the pilot is put into a situation their hands have not rehearsed. An order of practice matters more than the total number of flights. This guide combines the rules and checks that official sources state with a practice progression that is our own suggestion. The drills below are suggestions, not requirements, so adjust them to your aircraft and your local rules. We avoid fixed hour counts and speeds, because no source supports one number for every pilot.

## Key takeaways

- In the United States, recreational pilots must complete the TRUST test and carry proof, fly within visual line of sight or with a co-located visual observer, and stay at or below 400 feet in uncontrolled airspace, according to the FAA.
- The DJI Mini 4 Pro manual tells you to fly only in open areas, because tall buildings and large metal structures can affect the compass and GNSS accuracy, and not to fly in rain, fog or snow.
- The Mini 4 Pro specification lists a maximum wind resistance of 10.7 meters per second. A beginner should stay well below the limit of the aircraft.
- DJI recommends practicing in its Virtual Flight simulator before a first flight with an FPV aircraft such as the Avata.
- Build skills in order: pre-flight checks, a safe site, a steady hover, orientation control and only then new maneuvers.

## Step 1: Know the rules and run the checks

Before you practice, learn what applies where you live. For recreational flying in the United States, the FAA requires you to pass The Recreational UAS Safety Test (TRUST) and carry proof of passing. It requires you to keep the drone within visual line of sight, or use a visual observer who is next to you and in direct communication, and to fly at or below 400 feet in uncontrolled airspace. Our [beginner pre-flight checklist](/en/guides/beginner-drone-preflight-checklist) covers the checks before each flight, and the Mini 4 Pro manual adds the gimbal, the cameras and the sensors to its own list.

## Step 2: Choose a site that does not punish mistakes

The Mini 4 Pro manual says to fly only in open areas, because tall buildings and large metal structures can affect compass and GNSS accuracy and the home point may be less accurate when you take off near buildings. For a beginner that means a wide open space with no wires, cars, trees overhead or people nearby. The manual also tells you not to fly in rain, fog or snow. Start in calm weather, because the aircraft's wind limit is a limit, not a comfort level. Our [wind gust checklist](/en/guides/wind-gust-checklist-before-drone-flight) helps you judge gusts before you start.

## Step 3: Use a simulator where one exists

DJI describes its Virtual Flight app as a simulator for practicing flying techniques, and it recommends using it before a first flight with the Avata, since FPV flying differs from line-of-sight flying. The app supports the DJI FPV combo and the Avata. For a camera drone, check whether your manufacturer offers a practice mode or simulator. Time in a simulator lets the stick movements become automatic before a mistake can cost you the aircraft.

## Step 4: Learn to hold a steady hover

The most useful drill is the dull one. Take off, climb to a low, safe height, and try to hold the drone over one spot using small stick taps instead of large pushes. Almost every smoother maneuver, such as a gentle reveal or a precise landing, is a series of small corrections, and the habit begins here. Start in calm air, and only move on to light wind when the hover is calm. Stop and land if you lose control of the position.

## Step 5: Learn orientation

Stick inputs are relative to the drone's nose. When the aircraft is facing you, left and right look reversed, and this is a common reason for beginner crashes. A suggested drill is to fly a slow circle around yourself at a safe height and distance while you say out loud which direction is the drone's left and which is yours. Keep track of the nose direction using the front lights or the app's orientation icon. Keep the drone in your line of sight at all times.

## Step 6: Add one skill at a time

Do not attempt tricks, fast reveals or close orbits until the basics are automatic. A maneuver you can do when nothing goes wrong is different from one you can recover from when something does. Use automated modes carefully: the Mini 4 Pro manual warns against using MasterShots and QuickShots near buildings or where the GNSS signal is weak. Add a skill only when the previous one survives a distraction.

## Common mistakes

Flying in cluttered places, ignoring the checklist, choosing windy days because they look fine, and moving on to cinematic shots too soon are the usual patterns. Another is trusting return-to-home without understanding it; read our [return-to-home guide](/en/guides/return-to-home-settings-explained) before you depend on it.

## Practical checklist

- Learn your local rules, complete any required test and carry the proof.
- Run the pre-flight checks, including the gimbal, lenses and propellers.
- Choose an open site away from buildings, wires, people and traffic, in calm weather.
- Practice in a simulator if one exists for your aircraft.
- Master a steady hover, then orientation, then new maneuvers one at a time.
- Keep the aircraft in sight and stay within the legal height limit.

## FAQ

### How many hours should I practice before my first real flight?

We do not give a number. DJI recommends simulator practice before a first FPV flight, and the right amount depends on you and the aircraft.

### Where should I practice?

In an open area with no people, buildings or wires, as the Mini 4 Pro manual advises, in calm weather.

### Do I need a test to fly recreationally in the United States?

Yes. The FAA says recreational flyers must complete TRUST and carry proof.

### Why does the drone seem to move the wrong way?

Stick inputs are relative to the drone's nose. When the nose points toward you, left and right appear reversed.
