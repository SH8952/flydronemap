Every drone has a manufacturer-rated maximum wind resistance — the wind speed it's rated to fly in and still hold position. Here's a quick comparison of popular models, useful whether you're choosing a drone or judging whether today's wind is within your aircraft's limits.

## Why you shouldn't treat the rated number as your real flying limit

A manufacturer's max wind resistance figure is the point at which the aircraft can still hold its attitude under controlled test conditions — it's not a guarantee of stable footage or comfortable flying. As wind approaches that limit, battery drain accelerates, gimbal shake and footage jitter increase, and a sudden gust on top of sustained wind near the rating can momentarily overwhelm the aircraft's control authority.

## The safety margin experienced pilots actually use

Many experienced pilots treat **60-70% of the rated figure** as their practical ceiling. For a DJI drone rated at 38 km/h, that works out to roughly 23-27 km/h before they'd reconsider flying. Beginners should be even more conservative — staying under about 15-20 km/h while building experience.

## How the dashboard's "wind check for your drone" works

On the FlyDroneMap dashboard you can pick one of the models above and the current gust speed is compared with that model's official maximum wind resistance. Gusts **below 50% of the maximum show as good, 50% or more as caution, and 70% or more as not recommended**. For a model rated at about 43 km/h (12 m/s), for example, caution starts at around 22 km/h and "not recommended" at around 30 km/h. When a model lists different values for takeoff/landing and in flight, the lower one is used. The verdict is a reference based on ground-level gusts and does not take airspace or regulations into account.

## Before you fly

- Judge by gust speed, not the average wind reading.
- Confirm your exact model and its current official spec on the manufacturer's site.
- Treat the numbers here as a reference summary — your manufacturer's official spec is always the final word on safety limits.
