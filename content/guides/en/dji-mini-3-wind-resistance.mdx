At 248 g, the DJI Mini 3 is light enough that wind deserves a close look before every flight. DJI lists a maximum wind speed resistance of 10.7 m/s, about 38.5 km/h, and labels it Level 5. This page puts that number next to the rest of the specs, collects what DJI's documents say about wind, and shows where FlyDroneMap draws its own gust lines.

## What stands out

- The specs page gives 10.7 m/s (38.5 km/h), Level 5, with no test conditions.
- DJI's safety guidelines say not to use the aircraft in severe weather including heavy wind exceeding 10.7 m/s, and to land in a safe place when a high wind warning appears.
- The manual warns that the aircraft may be unable to return to its home point when the wind is too strong, and that a cold battery lowers wind resistance.
- On FlyDroneMap, gusts under 19.3 km/h are good for the Mini 3, 19.3 km/h and above are caution and 27.0 km/h and above are not recommended. The site chose these thresholds itself.

## How DJI talks about wind

Three DJI sources touch the topic, each from a slightly different angle.

The safety guidelines are the strictest: severe weather, including heavy wind above 10.7 m/s, is off limits, and a low battery or high wind warning means landing in a safe place. The user manual is about consequences. It says the aircraft may be unable to return to its home point in strong wind, and it states in its low-temperature battery warning that cold reduces wind speed resistance; see [temperature and drone battery performance](/en/guides/temperature-and-drone-battery-performance) for background. The FAQ is the gentlest. Because the drone is light, slight vibration is expected in strong wind, and the gimbal keeps adjusting to hold the picture steady.

## Where FlyDroneMap draws the lines

The dashboard compares the current gust with the model's official maximum. Good means under 50% of it, caution starts at 50% and not recommended starts at 70%. For a ceiling of 38.5 km/h that gives 19.3 and 27.0 km/h.

The dashboard's separate gust-risk scale is low under 20 km/h, moderate from 20 to under 35 km/h and high from 35 km/h. The model check asks how close a gust is to this aircraft's rating, whereas the gust scale says how rough the air is in general. For a Mini 3, 35 km/h is already about 91% of the official maximum.

## What the 10.7 m/s figure leaves out

The number is the manufacturer's maximum, and a light aircraft meets gusts as sudden pushes, so the sustained reading says little on its own. Forecast wind refers to 10 m above ground, and at 80–120 m the air can move faster; see [wind shear by altitude](/en/guides/wind-shear-by-altitude-and-drone-flight). A drone that goes out on a tailwind comes home into a headwind, which takes more power. DJI itself warns that return may fail in high wind, so keep a generous reserve and read [return-to-home settings explained](/en/guides/return-to-home-settings-explained). A cold battery narrows the margin further.

## Before the props spin

1. Open the [FlyDroneMap dashboard](/en) and read gusts for your location, plus the wind at 80 and 120 m.
2. Compare the gust with the bands above: under 19.3 km/h is good, from 19.3 km/h take care.
3. Go through the [wind and gust checklist](/en/guides/wind-gust-checklist-before-drone-flight) for direction, obstacles and the way home.
4. Use [wind resistance by model](/en/guides/drone-wind-resistance-by-model) if you fly more than one aircraft.
5. Check again just before takeoff, and land early if gusts increase or a high wind warning appears.

## Common mistakes

- Treating 10.7 m/s as a target.
- Reading the average wind and ignoring gusts.
- Expecting the advertised flight time in wind, when it was measured without any.
- Starting the return late or with too little battery for a headwind.
- Flying in the cold without allowing for a weaker battery.

## Questions and answers

### What is the wind limit of the DJI Mini 3?

DJI's specs page lists 10.7 m/s (Level 5), about 38.5 km/h, and its safety guidelines say not to use the aircraft in heavy wind exceeding 10.7 m/s.

### Can I fly the Mini 3 in 30 km/h wind?

30 km/h is about 8.3 m/s, roughly 78% of the official maximum, which puts it in FlyDroneMap's not-recommended range that starts at 27.0 km/h. If that is a gust, or the wind is stronger up high, the margin is smaller still.

### Does cold weather change the wind margin?

According to DJI's manual, a cold battery reduces the aircraft's wind speed resistance. No number is given, so allow extra margin in cold weather.
