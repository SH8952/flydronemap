Cold weather gets most of the attention in drone forums, but heat causes a different and in some ways more serious set of problems — from batteries that swell inside a hot car to hot motors and electronics that need airflow. Understanding why heat behaves so differently from cold changes how you prepare for a summer flight.

## Key takeaways

- Manufacturer manuals set an upper operating and charging limit of 40°C for the DJI models cited here, and some state that charging stops automatically when the battery is too hot.
- A parked vehicle in the sun heats up within minutes, and cracking the windows does not fix it, so never leave batteries or the aircraft in the car.
- Let a battery cool to near room temperature before charging, and never use a swollen or damaged battery.
- Keep vents clear, keep the aircraft in shade before launch, and stay alert for overheat warnings.

## What the manuals say about heat

The limits below are for the named DJI models. Other aircraft differ, so use these as examples and read your own manual.

| Source | Statement |
|---|---|
| DJI Mavic 3 user manual | Operating temperature is -10°C to 40°C. The charger stops charging if the cell temperature is outside 5°C to 40°C (41°F to 104°F). |
| DJI Mini 4K user manual | Charging stops automatically if the battery exceeds 50°C (122°F) during charging. Store the battery in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight. |
| DJI Mavic 3 Enterprise safety guidelines | Do not leave batteries near heat sources, under direct sunlight, or inside a vehicle in hot weather. |
| DJI Mavic Air battery safety guidelines | Do not leave batteries inside a vehicle on hot days. Never store the battery above 45°C or below 0°C. |

## Why sun matters more than air temperature

The air temperature on the weather page describes shade. A black battery, a dark aircraft shell or a dark case sitting in direct sun absorbs radiation and can be much hotter than the air around it. A battery that is already warm before takeoff has less margin left when the motors start drawing current. This is why the temperature shown on the FlyDroneMap site is a starting point, not a guarantee: on a bright day, keep the aircraft and batteries in shade until you are ready to launch.

## The hot-car mistake

Leaving a drone case or spare batteries in a parked car is one of the most common ways a flight starts already too warm. The US National Weather Service describes how fast vehicles heat: in the example it gives, a car reached an unsafe 94.3°F (about 35 °C) in just over two minutes, leaving the windows slightly open does not significantly reduce the heating rate, and a dark dashboard or seat can easily reach 180 to over 200°F (about 82 to over 93 °C). That page is about children and pets, but the physics applies equally to a battery on the dashboard. DJI's safety guidelines for the Mavic 3 Enterprise and Mavic Air also say not to leave batteries in a vehicle in hot weather.

## Onboard electronics and heat

Motors, the aircraft body and the camera all produce heat in flight. The DJI Mini 4K manual warns that the motors may be hot after flight and not to touch them, and the Mavic 3 manual says not to block the camera's ventilation holes because the heat generated may damage the device. In practice:

- Keep vents and openings clear, and do not seal an aircraft in a tight case straight after landing.
- Expect overheat or temperature warnings to appear more readily in hot weather. If your aircraft shows one, land and let it cool rather than waiting to see what happens.
- Give the aircraft time in shade before a long or demanding flight on a hot day.

This guide does not state exact thermal cut-off temperatures for any model, because we did not find them in the manuals reviewed. Look in your aircraft's manual or the app's warning descriptions.

## Charging and storage after a hot flight

DJI is consistent on this point. The Mavic 3 manual says not to charge an Intelligent Flight Battery immediately after flight because its temperature may be too high, and to wait until it cools down to room temperature. The Mavic 3 Enterprise guidelines add that charging a hot battery may cause serious damage to it. The same documents give 5°C to 40°C as the charging range, with 22°C to 28°C described as ideal. Do not charge in a parked car or in direct sun, and keep spare batteries in the shade between flights.

## Swollen or damaged batteries

A battery that looks puffy, has a case that no longer sits flat, or shows leaking should not be flown or charged. DJI's Mavic 3 Enterprise guidelines say not to use swollen, leaking or damaged batteries and to contact DJI or an authorized dealer, and the Mavic Air guidelines say never to use or charge them. The Mini 4 Pro maintenance manual adds that a damaged, swollen, leaking or flooded battery may rupture or catch fire and should not be used or transported. Heat accelerates wear but is not the only cause, so inspect every battery before every flight regardless of the weather. More on routine checks is in [drone battery care and lifespan tips](/en/guides/drone-battery-care-and-lifespan-tips).

## Practical hot-day checklist

- Keep batteries and aircraft in shade, not in a parked car or on a dashboard.
- Inspect every battery for swelling, leaks or a case that no longer sits flat before flight.
- Check that vents are clear and the aircraft is not sitting in direct sun before launch.
- Land and cool down if a temperature warning appears.
- Let batteries cool to near room temperature before charging, and charge only in the allowed range.
- Plan for moderate flight times and check wind and temperature for your site on the [FlyDroneMap dashboard](/en).

## FAQ

### What is the maximum safe temperature for flying a drone?

It depends on the model. The DJI Mavic 3 and Mini 4K manuals both give 40°C as the upper end of the operating or charging range, but you should read the manual for your own aircraft.

### Is it safe to leave my drone battery in the car in summer?

No. DJI's safety guidelines for the Mavic 3 Enterprise and the Mavic Air say not to leave batteries inside a vehicle in hot weather, and the National Weather Service notes that cracking the windows does not significantly slow heating.

### How long should a battery cool before charging?

DJI's wording is to wait until the battery cools down to room temperature, or close to it, rather than giving a fixed number of minutes. A battery that still feels warm is not ready.

### What should I do with a swollen battery?

Stop using it and do not charge or transport it. Contact the manufacturer or an authorized dealer, and follow local rules for disposal and transport.
