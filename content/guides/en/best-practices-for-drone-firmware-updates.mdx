Many pilots tap update as soon as a notification appears, the same way they would with a phone app. Firmware is different because it runs the aircraft itself, so an interrupted update is a bigger problem than a failed app update. This guide gives general habits, and it relies on your manufacturer's instructions for anything model-specific. We cite DJI's published pages as an example because they are widely used, and they do not apply to every brand.

## Key takeaways

- DJI says the DJI Fly app prompts you for firmware updates when the aircraft is connected to the remote controller, and its manual tells you to update whenever prompted.
- The requirements for battery level and process differ by model, so follow what the app and the manufacturer's release notes say for your aircraft.
- Do not start an update right before a flight that matters. Leave time to test.
- Keep power and connections stable until the update has finished.
- After an update, check your settings and run a short test flight, and keep the manufacturer's support contact for recovery steps.

## When to update

The manual of the DJI Mini 4 Pro says that a prompt appears in DJI Fly when new firmware is available, and recommends updating whenever you are prompted to get the best experience. That is a reasonable default for most flights. The practical exception is timing: if you have an important job or trip coming up, do the update days earlier, not the night before, so that you have time to test the aircraft and, if something is wrong, to contact support. A pilot with a second aircraft can update the spare first and check it before updating the main unit.

## Power and connection stability

The exact battery requirement varies by model and brand, and the pages we reviewed do not give a number for it, so use the figure the app or the manufacturer states. Whatever the number is, the principle is the same: a firmware update should not be interrupted. Charge the aircraft and the controller well, use the cable the manufacturer supplies or a short one of good quality, avoid hubs, and do not touch the cable or the power button while the app shows that an installation is in progress. If the app tells you to keep the aircraft powered or in a specific mode, follow it exactly.

## Keep aircraft, controller and app in step

When more than one update is offered, for the aircraft, the controller and the app, install them together as the app instructs. Different versions of components can lead to features that do not work or to connection problems, and the app's prompts are designed to bring the parts into line. If you cannot update one part, check the manufacturer's page before flying.

## Check your settings afterward

An update can change defaults, move menu items or add features. After any update, walk through the safety settings you rely on: the return-to-home altitude, the action on signal loss, height and distance limits and obstacle sensing. Compare them with what you used before the update, using your notes or a screenshot you took earlier. If the release notes mention a calibration, do it as described, in an open area away from metal and cables, and finish with a short hover test before a real flight. Our [return-to-home guide](/en/guides/return-to-home-settings-explained) explains why those settings matter.

## Updating more than one aircraft

If you operate several aircraft, update one first, test it and then update the others. A problem in a new release then affects one unit, not the whole fleet. Keep a note of the version each aircraft has and of the date you updated it.

## If an update stalls or fails

Do not repeatedly switch the aircraft off and on if the progress indicator seems stuck. Wait a reasonable time, then look at the manufacturer's support page for your model for a recovery procedure, and contact support if the aircraft does not respond to it. We cannot give model-specific recovery steps here, because they depend on the product.

## Practical checklist

- Update when prompted, but not right before an important flight.
- Use the battery level the app or manufacturer requires, and keep power and cables stable throughout.
- Update aircraft, controller and app together when asked.
- After the update, check return-to-home, signal-loss action, limits and obstacle sensing.
- Calibrate only as the app or release notes tell you, then do a short test flight.
- If something goes wrong, stop and use the manufacturer's recovery page or support.

## FAQ

### Should I always install the newest firmware?

DJI's manual recommends updating whenever you are prompted. Timing and testing are up to you, so avoid the day before an important flight.

### What battery level is needed?

It depends on the model, and the sources we checked do not state one. Follow the app and the manufacturer's notes for your aircraft.

### Can an update change my settings?

It can change defaults or add options, so check your safety settings after each update.

### What if the update gets stuck?

Do not retry blindly. Use the manufacturer's recovery procedure for your model, or contact support.
