Lithium-polymer (LiPo) batteries, used in nearly all consumer drones, are chemically sensitive to temperature — and cold is the more common problem pilots underestimate.

## Key takeaways

- Cold slows the chemical reactions inside the cell, which raises internal resistance and lowers the capacity and voltage you can actually draw.
- DJI's own manuals describe capacity as "significantly reduced" in the cold and give model-specific temperature limits, so check the manual for your aircraft.
- Warm the battery before flight, hover briefly after takeoff, and land with more reserve than you would in mild weather.
- The 20–30% figure in this guide's title is a cautious planning margin, not a number published by the manufacturers cited below.

## Why cold hurts LiPo batteries

Inside a battery, current is produced by chemical reactions that slow down as the cell gets colder. The practical result is higher internal resistance: the battery can deliver less capacity, and its voltage sags more when the motors demand power. DJI's winter-operations guidance for its enterprise aircraft puts the threshold for this at 15°C (59°F): below it, a battery's internal resistance increases and its discharge capacity falls, and DJI warns that a single cell dropping under 3 V causes power problems.

## What manufacturers say about temperature limits

The numbers below come from manuals for specific DJI models and are not universal. Other brands and other DJI models have different limits.

| Source | Statement |
|---|---|
| DJI Mavic 3 user manual | Operating temperature of the product is -10°C to 40°C. Battery capacity is significantly reduced when flying at -10°C to 5°C (14°F to 41°F), and DJI recommends hovering in place for a while to heat the battery. |
| DJI Mini 4K user manual | Operating temperature is 0°C to 40°C. Battery capacity is significantly reduced when flying at 0°C to 5°C (32°F to 41°F). |
| DJI Mavic Air battery safety guidelines | Use of the battery below -10°C can lead to permanent damage. |
| DJI enterprise winter guidance | Preheat the battery to over 15°C (59°F) before flight and hover for about one minute after takeoff to finish warming it. |

The same Mavic 3 and Mini 4K manuals say the charger only charges between 5°C and 40°C (41°F and 104°F). A battery that is too cold to fly comfortably may also be too cold to charge, so let it warm indoors before plugging it in.

## What this looks like in flight

A battery that shows 100% at takeoff in cold weather can trigger a critical low-battery warning or return-to-home far earlier than expected, because the aircraft reacts to what the cells can deliver under load, not only to the percentage on screen. Sudden altitude loss in the last minute of a cold flight is a known symptom of a cold, sagging battery. Gentle flying helps: DJI's winter guidance also advises avoiding long stretches of high speed at maximum thrust, which is the kind of demand a cold battery handles worst.

## How much shorter will the flight be?

None of the manufacturer pages cited here converts the loss into a percentage. They say "significantly reduced" and leave the rest to the pilot. The 20–30% in this guide's title is therefore best read as a conservative planning margin: if the aircraft is rated for a given flight time, plan the mission as if you will get noticeably less, and let the real battery behavior in your first short flight correct the plan. How much you actually lose depends on the model, the battery's age, the wind and how warm the pack was when you launched.

## A practical cold-weather battery routine

1. Check the manual for your model's operating range and do not fly outside it.
2. Keep spare batteries insulated, for example in an inner jacket pocket, until just before use.
3. Take off with a battery that has been near room temperature, not one that has sat in a cold car or bag.
4. After takeoff, hover close to the ground for a short time so the battery warms under load, as the DJI documents recommend, and watch the voltage and percentage.
5. Land with more margin than usual. Do not count on the last part of the indicated charge in cold conditions.
6. After landing, let the battery return to a moderate temperature before charging it, and charge only inside the allowed charging range.

## Common mistakes

- Trusting the percentage alone. A cold battery can look full and still sag when you climb or fight wind.
- Flying a battery straight out of a cold vehicle or a trunk.
- Pushing hard maneuvers in the first minute, before the pack has warmed.
- Charging a frozen battery or one that was just brought in from the cold. The charging window in the manuals above starts at 5°C.
- Setting the return-to-home height or reserve threshold for summer conditions and never revisiting it in winter.

## Heat matters too

At the other extreme, a battery that is too hot is also outside the manufacturer's range. The Mavic 3 and Mini 4K manuals give 40°C as the upper limit for operation and charging, and the Mini 4K manual says charging stops automatically if the battery exceeds 50°C. Details and precautions are in [extreme heat: drone battery and electronics precautions](/en/guides/extreme-heat-drone-battery-electronics-precautions).

## FAQ

### Does cold weather really shorten drone flight time?

Yes. Manufacturer manuals state that battery capacity is significantly reduced in cold conditions, and cold also increases voltage sag under load. The size of the loss depends on the model, the battery and its starting temperature.

### What is the lowest temperature I can fly at?

It depends entirely on your aircraft. The DJI Mavic 3 manual gives -10°C as the lower operating limit and the Mini 4K manual gives 0°C, so read the manual for your own model rather than relying on a general figure.

### Is hovering to warm up the battery a good idea?

DJI's Mavic 3 manual recommends hovering in place for a while to heat the battery in cold conditions, and its enterprise winter guidance suggests about one minute after takeoff. Do it low and in a safe spot, and stay ready to land if the battery warnings change.

### Can I charge a cold battery?

Check the charging range in your manual. The Mavic 3 and Mini 4K manuals say the charger only charges the battery between 5°C and 40°C, so a battery that is below that range should warm up first.
