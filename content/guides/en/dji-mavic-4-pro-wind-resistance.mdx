DJI gives the Mavic 4 Pro a maximum wind speed resistance of 12 m/s, or 43.2 km/h. That is where the manufacturer's rating stops, and a day with wind anywhere near it will not feel like the spec sheet. Below is what DJI documents, how FlyDroneMap grades a forecast gust against that figure, and a short routine for the last few minutes before takeoff.

## The short version

- DJI's specs page lists a maximum wind speed resistance of 12 m/s (43.2 km/h) for the Mavic 4 Pro.
- DJI's manual advises against flying in severe weather such as strong winds, snow, rain and fog. In the sections we read there is no numeric wind limit for flight.
- FlyDroneMap grades gusts for this model as good below 21.6 km/h, caution from 21.6 km/h and not recommended from 30.2 km/h. Those cut-offs are the site's own, not DJI's.
- Read the gust at the height you will fly, not the average at ground level.

## Three lines on the gust scale

Everything below hangs on three figures, all in km/h: 21.6, where FlyDroneMap's caution begins; 30.2, where not recommended begins; and 43.2, where DJI's rating ends. They are 50%, 70% and 100% of the official maximum. A forecast gust of 25 km/h is about 58% of the ceiling, so it lands in caution, while 32 km/h is about 74% and lands in not recommended.

The dashboard also keeps a separate gust-risk scale: low under 20 km/h, moderate from 20 to under 35 km/h and high from 35 km/h. The two answer different questions. One compares the wind with this aircraft; the other describes how rough the air is for anything that flies.

These rows come from DJI's Mavic 4 Pro specs page, and only rows the page lists are included. Several figures carry test conditions in DJI's footnotes, so read those before setting them beside your own flights.

## Wind in the manual

Four statements in DJI's manual bear on wind:

- Do not fly in severe weather such as strong winds, snow, rain and fog.
- If the wind is too strong, the aircraft may be unable to return to the Home Point.
- The one numeric wind figure we found applies to Return-to-Home when satellite positioning was unavailable at takeoff: the environmental wind speed should be below 3 m/s.
- A battery with reduced capacity in the cold lowers the aircraft's wind speed resistance. DJI gives no number for how much.

## Why a heavy aircraft still needs a margin

A rating of 12 m/s is a maximum, and a few things eat into it before you get there. Gusts arrive as short pushes above the average. Forecast wind is normally given at 10 m above ground, while you may fly at 80 to 120 m, where it can be noticeably stronger; [wind shear by altitude](/en/guides/wind-shear-by-altitude-and-drone-flight) explains why. A headwind costs battery too, and if you flew out with a tailwind, the way home is the hard part. Cold, finally, takes capacity from the battery and so from the wind rating.

## Three minutes before takeoff

1. Open the [FlyDroneMap dashboard](/en), choose the Mavic 4 Pro and read the gust figure rather than the average.
2. Check the wind at 80 and 120 m next to the surface value.
3. Go through the [wind and gust checklist](/en/guides/wind-gust-checklist-before-drone-flight) for direction and nearby obstacles.
4. Plan the flight so that the return leg has the wind behind you, or carry a generous battery reserve.
5. If you fly several aircraft, compare them in [wind resistance by model](/en/guides/drone-wind-resistance-by-model).

## Easy ways to get it wrong

- Taking 12 m/s as a comfortable speed rather than a maximum.
- Looking at the average and skipping the gust figure.
- Assuming the surface forecast describes the air at flight height.
- Launching on a cold morning with a cold battery and expecting full wind performance.
- Drifting far downwind without working out the way back.

## Questions and answers

### What is the wind limit of the DJI Mavic 4 Pro?

DJI's specs page lists a maximum wind speed resistance of 12 m/s (43.2 km/h). Its manual also advises against flying in severe weather such as strong winds.

### Can I fly the Mavic 4 Pro in 25 km/h wind?

25 km/h is about 58% of the 43.2 km/h maximum, which FlyDroneMap rates as caution. First check whether 25 km/h is a gust or an average, and what the wind is doing at flight height.

### Does cold weather change the wind limit?

DJI's manual says reduced battery capacity at low temperatures lowers the aircraft's wind speed resistance. It gives no figure, so treat a cold day as one with a thinner margin.
