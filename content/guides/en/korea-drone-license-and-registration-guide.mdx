The thing that trips up pilots in South Korea most often is that registration, a pilot certificate, and flight authorization are three separate approvals, not one bundled step. Pilots who check only one of the three and skip the others are the ones who get stopped mid-shoot. Here is the order in which they actually work, from the weight limits to the portal you file through.

## Weight decides, and not every limit means the same weight

Applications for drones in South Korea run through the Drone One-Stop portal (drone.onestop.go.kr): registration of the aircraft (Gichae-singo), flight authorization, the application for aerial photography, and a combined application for flight and photography. The portal's guide ties the registration duty to weight and seating criteria set by the transport ministry; for unmanned powered aircraft it names an empty weight of at most 150 kg without fuel. We could not confirm from the official pages we could access which lower limit applies to light drones. So check in the portal itself whether your aircraft has to be registered.

Note the difference between the weight figures. The portal's registration criteria refer to the aircraft's own weight (without fuel). The classes of the pilot certificate, by contrast, depend on the maximum takeoff weight. That is the highest weight at which the aircraft may take off, and an aircraft just under a limit can easily exceed it with an extra battery or accessories. Anyone who only compares the number on the spec sheet can therefore get it wrong. Check the manufacturer's figures and your actual weight with accessories.

## The pilot certificate depends on weight, not on intent

Toy aircraft up to 250 g are exempt from the pilot certificate; above that, the requirements and the examination procedure depend on the maximum takeoff weight. The certificate is divided into classes by weight. The lowest, Class 4, applies to aircraft above 250 g up to 2 kg maximum takeoff weight and is obtained through an online course and an online exam run by the Korea Transportation Safety Authority (KOTSA). The higher classes apply to heavier aircraft; the exact weight limits, the required flight experience and the exams can be found at KOTSA, and we do not reproduce them here. The minimum age is 14, and 10 for Class 4 with powered unmanned aircraft.

The common mistake is thinking "it's just a hobby drone, so I don't need a certificate." What counts is the weight of the aircraft, not the purpose of the flight. Even someone flying a camera drone purely for fun falls under the certificate system once the aircraft is heavier than 250 g.

## When a separate flight authorization is needed

Registration and a certificate do not cover everything. Flight authorization (Bihaeng-seungin) is an approval of its own. According to the Drone One-Stop guide, it is needed for flights at 150 m altitude or higher, for unmanned powered aircraft above 25 kg maximum takeoff weight regardless of altitude, and in flight restriction areas such as the surroundings of airports (control zones) and nuclear power plants. Where several airspaces overlap, the permissions of all responsible authorities apply. The responsible body is usually the transport ministry, and in certain zones the defense ministry. A park in a big city that looks wide open can still lie inside such a zone on the map, which is exactly why "it looks empty, it should be fine" is one of the most common causes of violations. Check the site beforehand with the portal's map function. The portal gives a processing time of about 3 working days for flight authorization; still, plan a generous buffer before your flight date, because the duration and the responsible body can vary by zone and altitude.

## Aerial photography needs its own application, separate from flight authorization

This is the step pilots forget most often: flight authorization does not automatically entitle you to take photos or film. According to the portal's guide, the application for aerial photography is a procedure of its own, which should be filed through the portal or the mobile app at least 4 working days before the shoot, so there is no room for a spontaneous same-day shoot. According to the guide, the confirmation of the application is valid for one year. Recording facilities of national security, military installations and other sensitive places is prohibited. We could not verify whether the confirmation is tied to a specific place or radius; before a shoot at a different location, ask in the portal whether a new application is needed.

## Common mistakes worth avoiding

- Using the weight from the spec sheet instead of the actual weight with batteries and accessories, and misjudging the registration or certificate duty as a result.
- Having flight authorization but skipping the separate application for aerial photography before shooting.
- Treating a quiet-looking area in a big city such as Seoul as unrestricted without checking whether it overlaps control or security airspace.
- Confusing the certificate duty with "hobby or commercial" instead of checking the weight limit.

## Practical checklist

- Determine the actual takeoff weight of your drone, with batteries and accessories, and check in the portal whether registration is required.
- Match the weight to the right certificate class (above 250 g up to 2 kg: Class 4) and clarify the requirements for heavier aircraft with KOTSA.
- Apply for flight authorization in good time through the Drone One-Stop portal if you will fly at 150 m or higher or in a restriction zone.
- If you want to take photos or film, file the separate aerial photography application at least 4 working days beforehand.
- Always check current requirements and final approval through the official Drone One-Stop portal (drone.onestop.go.kr).
