An MOA (Military Operations Area) is airspace designated for military aircraft to conduct training activities like combat maneuvers, aerial combat exercises, and high-speed low-altitude flying. Civilian aircraft aren't necessarily banned from flying there, but during active military training you'd be sharing that airspace with fast, unpredictable, low-flying military traffic.

## Why it matters

- Military aircraft maneuver at high speed, sometimes without the usual civilian air-traffic communication.
- Activation times are published in advance but can change, and are announced via NOTAM.
- Flying a drone inside an MOA isn't always illegal outright, but the real risk of encountering a fast, low-altitude military aircraft makes it something to avoid in practice.

## What pilots should know

- Check MOA boundaries on a map before you fly, and check NOTAMs or aviation information services for whether it's currently active.
- If the MOA is active, the safest choice is to avoid flying in or near it altogether.
- MOAs go by different names in different countries (training areas, danger areas, etc.), so check local terminology when flying abroad.
