Two pilots can fly the same battery and land with very different amounts of charge left. Most of that gap comes from conditions and habits, not from the battery itself. This guide explains the factors that change real flight time and uses the DJI Mini 4 Pro as a worked example, because DJI publishes its test conditions. We do not give fixed percentages for wind, speed or temperature, since they depend on the aircraft. Learn your own numbers from your flight log.

## Key takeaways

- A published flight time is a lab maximum. DJI states up to 34 minutes for the Mini 4 Pro with the Intelligent Flight Battery, measured in a windless wind tunnel at sea level while flying forward at a constant 21.6 km/h.
- Drag grows with the square of speed, according to NASA. Doubling your speed roughly quadruples drag, so fast flying costs battery.
- DJI says batteries drain faster in the cold. Below 5 degrees Celsius it advises warming the battery above 20 degrees first, then hovering for about a minute after takeoff.
- Gentle stick inputs help avoid battery voltage drops, according to DJI's winter flying tips.
- Plan the return leg with a margin, and trust the battery warnings of your own aircraft more than any number from a forum.

## Published times are measured in ideal conditions

Manufacturers measure maximum flight time under controlled conditions. DJI states that the Mini 4 Pro figure of 34 minutes was measured in a wind tunnel with no wind, at sea level, while flying forward at a constant 21.6 km/h with obstacle avoidance set to Brake and the camera in photo mode, until the battery forced a landing. DJI also lists a hovering time of 30 minutes in a windless lab. Your real flight includes climbs, turns, wind, filming and a reserve that you should not use, so expect a shorter usable time. The Intelligent Flight Battery Plus, sold in some countries, is listed with 45 minutes, but it also pushes the aircraft above 249 grams, which can change the rules that apply to you. A longer-lasting battery is not automatically the better choice.

## Speed, drag and smooth flying

NASA's drag equation shows that drag depends on the density of the air, the square of the velocity and the shape of the object. When speed doubles, drag grows about four times. For a multicopter that does not mean that flying slowly is always best, because the aircraft also spends power just to stay in the air. It means that fast flying costs more battery per kilometer, and that the manufacturer's measurement speed, 21.6 km/h in the Mini 4 Pro example, is a moderate cruise speed. When you do not need Sport mode, a calm cruise is a reasonable default. DJI's winter flying tips also advise pushing the control sticks gently to prevent battery voltage drops, which is the same idea: smooth inputs ask less of the battery than sudden ones.

## Wind changes the whole plan

Wind affects your ground speed and the power needed to hold a heading. The Mini 4 Pro specification lists a maximum wind speed resistance of 10.7 meters per second, and the manual tells you not to operate above it. Do not plan on the assumption that a headwind on the way out and a tailwind on the way back will cancel each other out. A practical habit is to fly into the wind first, so that the return leg is downwind and you find out early how much battery the outbound leg used. Always check the battery estimate at the turnaround point and return earlier than you planned if it is lower than expected. Our [wind gust checklist](/en/guides/wind-gust-checklist-before-drone-flight) explains how to read gusts before you take off.

## Cold weather and battery warm-up

DJI's winter flying page says the battery should be used between minus 10 and 40 degrees Celsius, that batteries drain faster in the cold, and that you should use only fully charged batteries. When it is below 5 degrees Celsius, DJI advises preheating the battery to above 20 degrees at room temperature, using a battery heater if your product has one, and hovering for about a minute after takeoff so that the battery can warm up. The same page says to check the battery status during the flight. Because DJI gives no capacity percentage, we do not either: in cold conditions, plan a shorter flight and watch the warnings. For general battery health, see our guides on [drone battery care and lifespan](/en/guides/drone-battery-care-and-lifespan-tips) and [temperature and battery performance](/en/guides/temperature-and-drone-battery-performance).

## Weight and accessories

Every gram you add has to be lifted. Extra accessories, a larger battery or a heavier camera setup can shorten flight time and change handling, and the effect depends on the aircraft. If you add something, fly a short test in a safe place and note the new flight time. Also remember that weight can matter for regulation: DJI states that the Mini 4 Pro weighs under 249 grams with the standard battery and that the Plus battery pushes it above that figure.

## Keep a reserve and use return-to-home settings

Do not fly the battery down to the last warning. Decide in advance at what percentage you will start the return, and make sure the return-to-home altitude and the low-battery behavior are set the way you expect. Our [return-to-home guide](/en/guides/return-to-home-settings-explained) explains the triggers on the Mini 4 Pro, including the final automatic landing that cannot be canceled.

## Practical checklist

- Treat the published flight time as a maximum, and learn your own real time from your flight log.
- Keep stick inputs smooth and use a calm cruise speed when you are not filming.
- Fly into the wind first so that the return leg is downwind, and check the battery at the turnaround.
- In the cold, use a fully charged and warmed battery, and hover for about a minute after takeoff.
- Add weight only after a short test flight, and note the effect on flight time.
- Start the return with a reserve, never at the last warning.

## FAQ

### Why is my real flight time shorter than the number in the specifications?

The number is measured in ideal conditions: no wind, constant speed, sea level and a specific camera mode. Real flights include wind, climbs, maneuvers and a reserve.

### Does flying slower always save battery?

Not necessarily. Drag falls as speed falls, but the aircraft also spends power to stay in the air. A moderate, steady speed with gentle inputs is a sensible default.

### How should I handle a cold day?

Follow your manufacturer. DJI advises preheating a battery above 20 degrees when it is below 5 degrees, hovering for about a minute and watching the battery status.

### Is the bigger battery always better?

Not always. It adds weight and can change which rules apply, as with the Mini 4 Pro and the 249 gram threshold.
