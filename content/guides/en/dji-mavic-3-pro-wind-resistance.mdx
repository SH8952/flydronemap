Anyone shopping for a stable camera drone will see the same line on the Mavic 3 Pro's specs page: maximum wind speed resistance, 12 m/s, which is 43.2 km/h. DJI sets that ceiling itself. This guide lays out where the figure comes from, what the manual adds to it, and how FlyDroneMap converts it into good, caution and not-recommended gust levels for this aircraft.

## Key takeaways

- The specs page gives 12 m/s (43.2 km/h) with no stated test condition.
- The user manual says not to operate the aircraft in severe weather, naming wind speeds exceeding 12 m/s, snow, rain and fog.
- For this model FlyDroneMap calls a gust under 21.6 km/h good, 21.6 km/h and above caution and 30.2 km/h and above not recommended. These are the site's thresholds, not an official standard.
- Gusts and wind at flight height matter more than the average near the ground.

## Two DJI documents, one number

The specs page and the manual agree on 12 m/s but do different jobs with it.

The manual adds that reduced battery capacity at low temperatures lowers the aircraft's wind speed resistance. DJI does not say by how much.

## Specs worth knowing

DJI states that the speed and flight time figures were measured in a wind tunnel under conditions equal to windless operation at sea level, and that real results vary with environment, use and firmware.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Max wind speed resistance | 12 m/s (43.2 km/h) |
| Takeoff weight | 958 g (Mavic 3 Pro Cine: 963 g) |
| Max horizontal speed | 21 m/s (75.6 km/h), measured in Sport mode |
| Max flight time | 43 minutes, wind tunnel test at a constant 32.4 km/h |
| Max takeoff altitude | 6000 m |
| Operating temperature | -10 to 40 °C |
| Obstacle sensing | Omnidirectional binocular vision system, plus an infrared sensor at the bottom |
| Satellite positioning | GPS, Galileo and BeiDou |

## Turning 12 m/s into gust levels

FlyDroneMap compares the forecast gust with the official maximum. The rule of thumb: good below 50% of the maximum, caution from 50%, not recommended from 70%. For the Mavic 3 Pro, 43.2 km/h gives these bands.

| Verdict | Share of 43.2 km/h | Gust speed |
|---|---|---|
| Good | Under 50% | Under 21.6 km/h (6.0 m/s) |
| Caution | 50% to under 70% | 21.6 to under 30.2 km/h |
| Not recommended | 70% or more | 30.2 km/h and above |

A separate gust-risk scale on the dashboard rates the air itself: low under 20 km/h, moderate from 20 to under 35 km/h, high from 35 km/h. It describes the conditions, not the aircraft.

## Why stay under the maximum

The rating is a maximum under the manufacturer's test conditions. Gusts are short peaks and the aircraft meets them as sudden pushes. Forecast wind usually refers to 10 m above ground, and at 80–120 m it can be stronger, as [wind shear by altitude](/en/guides/wind-shear-by-altitude-and-drone-flight) explains. A tailwind outbound means a headwind home, so battery reserve matters more than the outbound leg suggests. Cold lowers capacity, and DJI states that this reduces wind resistance.

## At the launch site

1. Pick the Mavic 3 Pro on the [FlyDroneMap dashboard](/en) and read the gust figure.
2. Read the wind at 80 and 120 m, not only the surface.
3. Work through the [wind and gust checklist](/en/guides/wind-gust-checklist-before-drone-flight), including direction and obstacles.
4. Plan to finish with the wind helping you, or keep a generous battery reserve.
5. Use [wind resistance by model](/en/guides/drone-wind-resistance-by-model) if you fly more than one aircraft.

## Slips that cost batteries

- Reading 12 m/s as a safe flying speed.
- Using the average wind and ignoring the gust number.
- Trusting the surface forecast for the air at flight height.
- Expecting full wind performance with a partly cold battery.
- Flying far downwind with no plan for the return.

## FAQ

### What is the wind limit of the DJI Mavic 3 Pro?

DJI's specs page lists a maximum wind speed resistance of 12 m/s (43.2 km/h), and the manual advises against operating in severe weather including wind speeds exceeding 12 m/s.

### Can I fly the Mavic 3 Pro in 30 km/h wind?

30 km/h is 69% of the 43.2 km/h maximum, so FlyDroneMap rates it as caution, just under the not-recommended line at 30.2 km/h. Confirm that 30 km/h is a gust rather than an average and look at the wind at flight height.

### Does the manual give separate takeoff and landing limits?

Not in the parts we read. The manual's wind guidance is the severe-weather warning, the Return to Home caveat and the cold-battery note.
