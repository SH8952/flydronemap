A FRIA (FAA-Recognized Identification Area) is a specific, designated area in the US where drones that don't broadcast Remote ID can still legally fly. Community-based organizations (CBOs) or educational institutions apply for FAA approval to establish one.

## Key takeaways

- A FRIA is a defined geographic area where a drone without Remote ID equipment may be flown.
- Both the drone and the pilot must stay inside the FRIA's boundaries for the whole flight, and the pilot must keep the drone in visual line of sight.
- Only FAA-recognized community-based organizations and educational institutions can apply, through FAADroneZone. Individual pilots cannot create a FRIA on their own.
- A FRIA is recognized for a limited term and can be terminated, so check the FAA's map rather than relying on an old list of flying sites.

## Why it exists

Once Remote ID became mandatory, drones that cannot broadcast it needed a lawful place to keep flying. A FRIA is the FAA's answer: inside the approved boundary, a drone without Remote ID is allowed to operate. The rules sit in 14 CFR Part 89, Subpart C, and the FAA explains the process in Advisory Circular 89-3.

If your drone does broadcast Remote ID (built in or through a broadcast module), you don't need a FRIA. See the [Remote ID requirements guide](/en/guides/remote-id-requirements-explained) for how the basic requirement works.

## How a FRIA works

| Point | What the FAA says |
|---|---|
| Who may fly without Remote ID | Drones without Remote ID equipment, only inside the FRIA |
| Where the pilot must be | The FAA states that both the drone and the pilot must be inside the FRIA boundaries throughout the operation |
| Line of sight | Drones without Remote ID must be flown within visual line of sight |
| Who may apply | FAA-recognized community-based organizations, and educational institutions (primary and secondary schools, trade schools, colleges and universities) |
| Where to apply | FAADroneZone, using the FRIA service option |
| Recognition term | 48 calendar months from FAA approval; renewal requests are due no later than 120 days before expiration |
| Termination | The FAA may terminate a FRIA, for example over aviation or public safety concerns or false or misleading information |

Applicants must identify the organization and its authorized representative, give the exact location, state the purpose and need for the area, and include any existing letter of agreement about the flying site. The FAA weighs overlapping airspace restrictions, the safe use of airspace by other aircraft, ground safety, and whether nearby FRIAs already meet the need (14 CFR 89.210 and 89.215).

## What pilots should know

- A FRIA is specific to the US FAA system. Other countries' equivalents, if any, use different names and conditions.
- Outside a FRIA, a drone without Remote ID can't be flown under the US rule, so check first whether your aircraft supports Remote ID.
- Being inside a FRIA does not cancel other airspace rules. The FAA notes that pilots remain responsible for understanding their airspace and getting any needed authorization. See the [US airspace classes guide](/en/guides/understanding-us-airspace-classes) and the [US regulations page](/en/regulations/us).
- The FAA publishes approved FRIA locations on an interactive map in its UAS Data Delivery System. Check the club or site yourself as well, since a FRIA is run by the organization that applied for it.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming any open field or club field is a FRIA. Only an area the FAA has recognized counts.
- Standing outside the boundary and flying the drone over it. The pilot must be inside as well.
- Treating a FRIA as a way around line-of-sight rules. Visual contact is still required.
- Forgetting that recognition expires after 48 months unless renewed, so a site that was a FRIA in the past may no longer be one.

## FAQ

### Who can create a FRIA?
Only FAA-recognized community-based organizations and educational institutions can apply. Individual pilots can't, but they can join a club that holds one.

### Do I need a FRIA if my drone has Remote ID?
No. A FRIA exists for drones that do not broadcast Remote ID. A drone that broadcasts it is covered by the standard Remote ID requirement instead.

### Can I fly a drone without Remote ID outside a FRIA?
Not under the FAA's Remote ID rule as described on its FRIA page; the drone must be flown within the FRIA. Check the FAA's Remote ID pages for the current rules and any other compliance routes.

### How do I find a FRIA near me?
Use the FAA's interactive FRIA map in the UAS Data Delivery System, then contact the organization that operates the site to confirm access and rules.
