A FRIA (FAA-Recognized Identification Area) is a specific, designated area in the US where drones that don't broadcast Remote ID can still legally fly. Community-based organizations (CBOs) or educational institutions apply for FAA approval to establish one.

## Why it exists

Once Remote ID became mandatory, older aircraft and homebuilt drones that don't support it were effectively grounded almost everywhere. A FRIA carves out an exception within a defined area so those aircraft can keep flying for recreational and hobby purposes.

## What pilots should know

- A FRIA is specific to the US FAA system — other countries' equivalent programs, if any, may use different names and conditions.
- Outside a FRIA, a drone without Remote ID can't legally fly, so check whether your aircraft supports Remote ID first.
- The FAA's official map shows nearby FRIA locations, and most are flying fields run by local drone or model-aircraft clubs.
