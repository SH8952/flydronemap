Return-to-Home, or RTH, does not bring your drone back by magic. It brings the drone back the way you configured it. If the configured altitude is lower than an obstacle on the way, or the home point is wrong, RTH can create the problem it was meant to solve. This guide uses the DJI Mini 4 Pro user manual as its main reference and notes where behavior is specific to that aircraft. Other models and brands differ, so read your own manual.

## Key takeaways

- On the Mini 4 Pro, RTH can start in three ways: you trigger it, the battery is low, or the control signal between the remote controller and the aircraft is lost.
- The manual tells you to set a suitable maximum altitude and RTH altitude before takeoff. The RTH altitude should be higher than all obstacles in the flying area.
- The first position where the aircraft gets a strong or moderate GNSS signal is saved as the default home point. A weak signal does not update the home point.
- For signal loss you can choose RTH, landing or hovering in the safety settings. Failsafe RTH starts after the signal has been lost for more than six seconds if the home point is registered and the compass works normally.
- The manual says not to take off from moving objects such as cars, ships and aircraft.

## The three ways RTH starts

According to the manual, RTH can be started by the pilot, by a low battery or by the loss of the control signal. When you trigger it yourself you can usually cancel it with the RTH button or the pause button on the controller. For a low battery, the aircraft estimates whether the remaining charge is enough to get home, based on its position, surroundings and speed, and shows a warning in DJI Fly. If you take no action, it returns automatically after a countdown. The manual warns that canceling after the warning may leave too little battery for a safe landing. When the battery is only enough to descend from the current altitude, the aircraft lands automatically, and that landing cannot be canceled, although you can still steer horizontally and control the descent rate. Because behavior differs by model, do not assume your aircraft works the same way.

## RTH altitude is a setting you must choose

The manual tells you to set a suitable RTH altitude before flight and lists it in its pre-flight checklist. It does not state a default value in the parts we could read, and a third-party guide for the same aircraft says only that the RTH altitude should be higher than all obstacles in the flying area. The practical rule follows: find the tallest obstacle on your route, such as trees, buildings, towers or the deck of a bridge, and set the RTH altitude above it with a margin that you decide for the site. Check that value before every flight, because it is easy to leave a setting from a previous location. Do not assume a number from this page or from a forum is correct for your aircraft.

## Home point recording

The Mini 4 Pro manual says the first location where the aircraft receives a strong or moderately strong GNSS signal, shown by a white icon, is saved as the default home point. It can be updated before takeoff if a new strong or moderate signal is received, and it is not updated if the signal is weak. You can also update it manually in the safety settings. Wait for the app to show that the home point has been recorded before you launch, and if you move to a different takeoff spot, check it again. The third-party guide notes that the pilot hears a voice prompt when the home point is updated.

## Signal loss

In the safety settings you can set what the aircraft does when the controller signal is lost: RTH, land or hover. If it is set to RTH, the home point is registered and the compass works normally, failsafe RTH starts after the signal has been lost for more than six seconds. In low light the same third-party guide says that after failsafe RTH the aircraft flies backward 50 meters and obstacle sensing is not available, so the altitude you set matters even more. Think about which action suits your site before you fly. Over water, forests or crowded areas the right choice may differ.

## Do not take off from moving objects

The manual says not to take off from moving objects such as cars, ships and aircraft. The reasoning is easy to follow: RTH flies to the recorded point, and a boat or vehicle will have moved on by the time RTH starts. If a flight from a moving platform is unavoidable, check your model's manual first and plan a manual return.

## GPS accuracy and space weather

RTH depends on satellite positioning. When GNSS quality is poor, the home point and the return path are less reliable. Check the Kp index and the satellite status in the app before the flight, as described in our [Kp index guide](/en/guides/what-is-the-kp-index), and keep a manual return plan ready.

## Practical checklist

- Set the maximum altitude and the RTH altitude for the site before takeoff, with the tallest obstacle in mind.
- Wait for the home point to be recorded and confirm it in the app.
- Choose the signal-loss action deliberately: RTH, land or hover.
- Know that a low-battery warning can be canceled but that the final automatic landing cannot.
- Do not take off from moving vehicles, boats or aircraft.
- Read your own model's manual, because behavior varies, and keep visual line of sight.

## FAQ

### What is the default RTH altitude?

The manual passages we could read do not state it, so check your own aircraft's settings and set the value yourself.

### When does the aircraft return by itself?

When the pilot triggers RTH, when the battery is low and no action is taken after the countdown, and when the control signal is lost, depending on the signal-loss action you set.

### Can I cancel RTH?

For pilot-triggered and low-battery RTH, the manual says you can cancel with the RTH or pause button. The final automatic landing cannot be canceled.

### Is RTH enough on its own?

No. It is a backup. Keep the aircraft in sight and plan a manual return.
